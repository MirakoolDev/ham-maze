Mode 1 · Playable now

The daily run

The maze from the Unity game, rebuilt for the browser in first person. It changes at 00:00 UTC and everyone gets the same layout, so the leaderboard is a fair fight.

Same maze for everyone

12 × 12 cells, 7 m each, generated from the date with the same algorithm as the Unity build. 143 openings, one route that matters.

Sentries guard it

Eight floating sentries patrol. They hunt on sight or on the sound of a shot, part their shells and fire. A hit to the eye does 45 damage, the body 20.

Your time is your score

The clock starts on your first metre and stops when you touch the monolith. Fastest three times split the day’s pot.

Mode 2 · Proposed

Maze Royale

Four players start in the four corners of the same maze. The sentries are still there. The first to touch the monolith wins, and anyone still standing when the others fall wins too.

  1. 1Join a lobby

    Pay the seat fee. A lobby holds four players.

  2. 2Wait up to 60 seconds

    Seats still empty when the timer ends are filled by bots, so a match always starts.

  3. 3Spawn in a random corner

    Corners are drawn with onchain randomness, so nobody can pick the short route.

  4. 4Race, dodge, fight

    Sentries patrol, crates hold power-ups, and you can shoot your rivals.

  5. 5The haze closes in

    Every 40 seconds the outer ring of cells fills with haze that drains health, pushing everyone toward the centre.

  6. 6Monolith or last alive

    First touch wins the match. If everyone else is down, the last player standing wins.

    Freeze

    The nearest rival in your line of sight stops dead for 3 seconds.

    Steal

    Take the power-up a rival is holding. If they hold nothing, take 10 of their rounds.

    See every rival on your compass for 6 seconds. Once per match.

    Shield

    Keeping it fair

    A time has to be proven before it pays

    A browser can be made to report any time it likes. A run only counts once a referee has replayed it and signed the result.

    Replay-checked runs

    The game records your inputs. The referee replays them in the same deterministic simulation, checks the finish time and signs it. The contract only accepts signed times.

    Server-run matches

    Royale matches run on an authoritative room server. It uses Net Forge, the engine behind Sherwood, which already fills empty seats with bots.

    Fair corners and crates

    Spawn corners and crate drops come from Dice, the same randomness service Sherwood uses.

    What you trust

    The referee’s signing key. Replays are published so anyone can check a winning run themselves.

    Funding the build

    The season pass

    A season pass NFT pays for development and gives holders the holder bonus, a free daily attempt, extra paint colours for the Marker, season challenges and early access to Maze Royale. Selling passes is how the build gets funded.

    Open questions

    • Entry price. What should one attempt and one royale seat cost?
    • Top 3 or top 10. ham2d pays the top ten; this page proposes the top three, as in Sherwood.
    • ETH or a token. Pay in ETH, or route entries through a token pool the way Sherwood uses $PFWA?
    • Paint. Should rivals see your Marker paint? Seeing it lets you follow a leader, or get misled by fake trails.
    • Player damage. A royale where players shoot each other, or a pure race where only sentries shoot?
